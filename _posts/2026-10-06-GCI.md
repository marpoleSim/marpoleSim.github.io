The Grid Convergence Index (GCI) is a standardized method to estimate CFD simulation error. It is also used to determine whether the solution is mesh independent. This method is formalized in ASME V&V 20.

## Assumption ##

For a continuous and infinitely differentiable function $\phi(x)$, a Taylor series expansion is, 

$$
\phi(x+h) = \phi(x) + \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} h + \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x^2} \frac{h^2}{2!}+...+\frac{\partial^p \phi}{\partial x^p} \frac{h^p}{p!}+0(h^{p+1})
$$

It is assumed that the CFD solution $\phi$ (a scalar solution such as pressure drop) can be approximated by Taylor series expansion.  

$$
\phi(h) = \phi + A_1 h + A_2 h^2+...+A_p h^p+0(h^{p+1})
$$

Or, in general,

$$
\phi(h) = \phi + C \cdot h^p+0(h^{p+1})
$$

$\phi$ is the theoritical solution. It is an approximation when $h \rightarrow 0$. 

## Richardson Extrapolation ##

Using two solutions $\phi_1$, $\phi_2$ with two different grid size $h_1$, $h_2$,

$$
\phi_1 \approx \phi + A_p {h_1}^p
$$

$$
\phi_2 \approx \phi + A_p {h_2}^p
$$

By adjusting the terms, 

$$
\frac{\phi_2 - \phi}{\phi_1 - \phi}=(\frac{h_2}{h_1})^p=r^p
$$

where, $$r=\frac{h_2}{h_1}$$

We then got the Richardson extrapolation. 

$$
\phi = \phi_1 + \frac{\phi_1 - \phi_2}{r^p-1}
$$

## Estimated Error ##

From Richardson extrapolation, the estimated discretization error of the fine grid solution $\phi_1$, because $\phi_1 \approx \phi$,

$$
E = \frac{\phi-\phi_1}{\phi} \approx \frac{\phi_1-\phi_2}{\phi_1 (r^p-1)}
$$

## Roache Approach  ##

The Roache approach uses solutions $(\phi)$ of three meshes (fine, medium and coarse). Again, the solution can be pressure drop, drag coefficient $C_D$, lift coefficient $C_L$ etc.

### 1. Representative Cell Size $(h)$ ###

The average representative grid size $h$ is estimated from total volume $V$ and cell count $N$.

$$
h \approx (\frac{V}{N})^{1/3}
$$

### 2. Refinement Ratio $(r)$ ###

The grid refinement ratio,

$$
r_{21}=\frac{h_2}{h_1}, \quad r_{32}=\frac{h_3}{h_2} 
$$

ASME V&V 20 requires the refinement ratio to be larger than 1.3. For simplicit, it can be set constant: $r_{21}=r_{32}=r$

### 3. Solution Differences $(\epsilon)$ ###

The solution differences are,

$$
\epsilon_{21}=\phi_2 - \phi_1, \quad \epsilon_{32}=\phi_3 - \phi_2
$$

### 4. Order of Accuracy $(p)$ ###

From the Taylor series approximation,

$$
\phi_1 = \phi + C h_1^p
$$

$$
\phi_2 = \phi + C h_2^p
$$

$$
\phi_3 = \phi + C h_3^p
$$

The errors,

$$
\epsilon_{21} = \phi_2 - \phi_1 = C(h_2^p-h_1^p)=Ch_1^p (r_{21}^p-1)
$$

$$
\epsilon_{32} = \phi_3 - \phi_2 = C(h_3^p-h_2^p)=Ch_2^p (r_{32}^p-1)
$$

This leads to,

$$
\frac{\epsilon_{32}}{\epsilon_{21}} = r_{21}^p \frac{r_{32}^p-1}{r_{21}^p-1}=r^p
$$

The apparent order of accuracy is estimated,

$$
p = \frac{\ln |\epsilon_{32} / \epsilon_{21}|}{\ln(r)}
$$

### 5. Estimated Error ###

Calculate the estimated error using solutions of the fine grid and the medium grid. 

$$
E_{21} = \frac{\phi_1-\phi_2}{\phi_1 (r^p-1)}
$$

### 6. Grid Convergence Index (GCI) ###

The grid convergence index (GCI) is defined,

$$
GCI = F_s \cdot E_{21} \times 100 \%
$$

Where $F_s$ is the safety factor. $F_s=1.25$ when using 3 or more grid. $F_s=3.0$ when using only 2 grids. For an engineering simulation, a criterion of $GCI < 1 \%$ is usually used.

### 7. Metrics ###

#### Convergence Ratio $(R)$ ####

$$
R=\frac{\epsilon_{21}}{\epsilon_{32}}
$$

- $0 < R < 1$: monotonic convergence
- $-1 < R < 0$: Oscillatory convergence
- $R > 1$ or $R<-1$: Divergence

#### Asymptotic Ratio $(AR)$ ####

$$
AR = \frac{GCI_{32}}{r^p\cdot GCI_{21}}
$$

When asymptotic ratio is close to 1.0, the grid refinement is within the asymtotic range. 

#### CFD result error ####

The CFD result error can be estimated with the formula below,

$$
\phi = \phi_1 \pm (\phi_1 \cdot GCI_{21})
$$

### 8. Report ###

It might not be accurate to say the solution is mesh independent. It is about the CFD solution error. It would be appropriate to use the wording "the solution prediction is mesh independent within $1\%$".  

It is also important to state this for a particular quantity of interest. For example, it might be accurate for pressure drop but might not be accurate for the total force.
